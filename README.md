#### How to use it
- Map: Displaus Zip code and Postal regions of Montgomery County of Maryland. 
- Dropbox: Allows users to filter regions by different accident severity to create a choropleth map.
- Checkbox: Option to toggle the visibility of accident locations on the map.
- Legend: Displays the color scale of choropleth map.
- Map Tooltips: Hover over any postal region details the counts of accident happend in that region.
- Location tooltips:Hover over any location icon details the Light, Collison Type, Vehicle make, Vehicle Model, Severity.

**Title: Understanding Traffic Accidents in Montgomery County: Severity and Location**

### **Introduction**
This visualization explores traffic accident data in Montgomery County, Maryland, focusing on accident severity and location. The goal is to uncover patterns to improve road safety.

### **Act 1: Severity of Injuries**
The map shows that postal areas tend to have frequent accidents with moderate injuries,and while other postal areas have fewer accidents but higher severity, indicating the need for targeted safety measures.

### **Act 2: Accident Locations**
The map reveals certain postal areas as accident hotspots, where accidents occur more frequently. These locations often correlate with high-traffic roads or complex intersections. Identifying these hotspots can help local authorities prioritize safety measures such as traffic signal improvements and better signage.

### **Act 3: Injury Breakdown**
The map categorizes accidents by injury severity, revealing areas with high fatality rates that may require better safety interventions, while areas with lower severity suggest effective safety measures.

### **Act 4: Accident Type and Vehicle Involvement**
Icons on the map identify accident locations and vehicle types, showing that distracted driving due to weather or light conditon and larger vehicles play a significant role in accidents.

**Title: Exploring Road Accidents: Light, Weather, and Driver Faults**
Introduction
The visualization focuses on how environmental factors (light and weather) and driver behavior influence road accidents. The goal is to uncover insights about:

Common vs. rare conditions contributing to accidents.
Whether drivers are generally at fault or not in these scenarios.
The interplay between surface conditions and accidents in different weather and light settings.


**Act 1: Drivers at Fault vs. Not at Fault**
The stacked bar charts provide a clear comparison:
Common Conditions: Under normal weather (e.g., clear skies, daylight), the charts may show a relatively high number of accidents where drivers are at fault, indicating possible negligence or overconfidence.
Rare Conditions: During rare or extreme weather (e.g., snow, severe winds), the balance might shift. A significant portion of accidents might not be the driver's fault, emphasizing the challenges of navigating such conditions safely.
Story Angle:
Accidents are heavily influenced by both human factors and environmental challenges.

**Act 2: The Role of Light Conditions**
The stacked segments for light conditions highlight:
Dark conditions (with/without lighting): Likely show higher accident rates due to reduced visibility.
Dawn/Dusk: Transitional periods could present moderate risks, with drivers struggling to adapt to lighting changes.
Daylight: Accidents in daylight often hint at human error more than environmental factors.
Story Angle:
Visibility is a key factor in road safety. While poor lighting is inherently risky, human behavior in daylight remains a significant concern.

**Act 3: Surface Conditions and TreeMap Insights**
The treemap digs deeper into surface conditions in specific scenarios:
Under clear weather with good lighting, accidents may disproportionately occur on dry or wet surfaces, suggesting distractions or speeding.
In extreme conditions (e.g., snow or rain), surface types like icy or muddy roads dominate, implying that infrastructure and preparedness are critical factors.
Story Angle:
Surface conditions act as amplifiers to weather and light challenges, and certain conditions call for better road maintenance and preparedness.

**Act 4: Behavioral Insights and Recommendations**
Interactive elements allow deeper exploration:
Selecting specific conditions (e.g., rain + dark) can show clusters of accidents involving drivers at fault.
This could be tied to behavioral issues like driving too fast for conditions or failing to use headlights.
Story Angle:
The visualizations highlight the need for targeted safety interventions:

**Public awareness campaigns focusing on driver caution during poor visibility.**
Infrastructure improvements, like better street lighting or timely snow/ice clearance.
Conclusion: A Call to Action
The visualization emphasizes that while drivers play a key role in road safety, infrastructure, weather patterns, and light conditions are significant contributors. Solutions must be multi-faceted, combining driver education, better technology (e.g., adaptive headlights), and robust infrastructure maintenance.

Final Message:
"By understanding accident patterns, we can pave the way for safer roads, no matter the weather or time of day."
